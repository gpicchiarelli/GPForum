GPForum - Human-Centered Community Lifecycle Constitution

This document defines the mandatory community lifecycle, emotional usability,
social continuity, engagement ergonomics, contributor identity, retention,
discovery, personalization, and long-term human-centered operation doctrine of
GPForum.

This constitution is mandatory.

It transforms GPForum from infrastructure-first forum architecture into durable
human-centered community platform architecture without compromising:

* PostgreSQL-first authoritative storage;
* Perl-first implementation;
* SSR-first rendering;
* explicit governance;
* operational simplicity;
* auditability;
* rebuildable projections;
* moderation authority;
* permission-safe rendering;
* graceful degradation;
* anti-dark-pattern philosophy.

GPForum explicitly rejects:

* manipulative engagement systems;
* addictive dark patterns;
* opaque behavioral ranking;
* hostile UX;
* attention-maximization incentives;
* infinite-scroll addiction architecture;
* exploitative gamification;
* hidden ranking manipulation.

GPForum preserves:

* explainability;
* operational clarity;
* moderation authority;
* privacy boundaries;
* accessibility;
* archival durability.

---

1. Human-Centered Community Philosophy

Forums are social memory systems.

Community continuity matters.

Emotional usability matters.

Readability matters.

Contributor recognition matters.

Long-form discussion matters.

Healthy return behavior matters.

Durable participation matters.

Software quality includes emotional ergonomics.

Communities require continuity signals.

Users SHOULD feel welcomed, oriented, and remembered.

Usability is architectural.

Retention MUST emerge from value, continuity, trust, and belonging, not from
addiction loops.

GPForum MUST prohibit:

* manipulative notification systems;
* artificial urgency loops;
* exploitative dopamine mechanics;
* dark-pattern engagement systems;
* hidden ranking manipulation;
* ragebait amplification;
* deliberately compulsive interaction loops.

2. Community Lifecycle Model

GPForum MUST model community participation as a lifecycle.

Mandatory lifecycle stages:

* anonymous visitor;
* newcomer;
* participant;
* regular;
* trusted contributor;
* moderator;
* long-term steward.

Anonymous visitors need:

* readable public-safe pages;
* clear context;
* no dark-pattern registration pressure;
* privacy-safe discovery.

Newcomers need:

* orientation;
* etiquette visibility;
* low-friction first participation;
* safe beginner discovery;
* validation errors that reduce anxiety.

Participants need:

* clear reply and quote flows;
* notification control;
* bookmarks and continuity;
* permission-safe feedback.

Regulars need:

* continuity;
* subscription-aware feeds;
* profile identity;
* contribution history;
* personalization.

Trusted contributors need:

* earned permissions where policy allows;
* visible recognition;
* explainable trust state;
* reversible moderation authority boundaries.

Moderators need:

* humane queues;
* audit-backed actions;
* escalation flows;
* sustainable workload surfaces.

Long-term stewards need:

* governance continuity;
* community health visibility;
* archival durability;
* policy evolution tools.

GPForum SHOULD support newcomer onboarding flows, contextual help, orientation
systems, contributor milestones, guided participation, and re-entry flows for
returning users.

20. Mandatory Vs Optional Community Features

Mandatory MVP posture:

* readable thread experience;
* stable anchors;
* basic profiles;
* replies and quotes;
* bookmarks or saved state where implemented;
* unread/read continuity;
* notification preference control;
* permission-aware discovery;
* moderation visibility;
* accessibility-preserving mobile behavior.

Strongly recommended:

* newcomer onboarding;
* continue-reading;
* contributor milestones;
* profile cards;
* related discussions;
* digest summaries;
* low-noise defaults;
* moderator wellness tooling.

Optional advanced:

* custom badges;
* trust levels;
* recommended discussions;
* solved threads;
* wiki posts;
* knowledge-base mode;
* PWA offline drafts;
* ActivityPub experiments.

Experimental:

* personalized discovery;
* community health dashboards;
* social presence indicators;
* collaborative editing;
* advanced summaries.

Experimental systems require ADR review when they affect privacy, ranking,
moderation, reputation, federation, or retention.

Phase 1: Reading and return continuity.

* Stable anchors.
* Unread tracking.
* Jump/resume behavior.
* Keyset-friendly long-thread navigation.

Phase 2: Contributor identity.

* Public-safe profiles.
* Contribution summaries.
* Role flair and moderation-safe identity decoration.

Phase 3: Discovery and feeds.

* Latest discussions.
* Followed discussions.
* Subscription-aware feeds.
* Permission-aware related threads.

Phase 4: Comfort and personalization.

* Theme and density preferences.
* Notification preferences.
* Mutes.
* Saved reading state.

Phase 5: Stewardship and health.

* Onboarding.
* Community digests.
* Moderator workload visibility.
* Healthy re-engagement.

Phase 6: Advanced knowledge systems.

* Solved threads.
* Curated indexes.
* Wiki posts.
* Knowledge-base views.

The correct GPForum community architecture is not the one that maximizes daily
clicks.

The correct architecture is the one that helps people return, understand,
contribute, care for each other, preserve knowledge, and leave without being
manipulated.
